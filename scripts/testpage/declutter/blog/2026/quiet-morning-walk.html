A quiet morning walk along the river

By Jun Park

The fog had not lifted when I reached the river path at six. The only sounds were the water against the stones and a heron that lifted off as I came around the bend. I walk this stretch most mornings, and it is never the same twice.

In spring the banks are full of wild garlic, and the smell follows you for a mile. By late September the garlic is long gone and the willows are starting to yellow at the tips. The path is soft underfoot after a week of rain, and the puddles hold the grey of the sky.

I passed two runners and a man with an old spaniel who stops at every bench. We nodded to each other, as we have for years, without ever learning each other's names. There is a kind of friendship in that, built on nothing more than the same hour and the same path.

Near the footbridge the fog began to thin, and the far bank came into view: the boathouse, the row of poplars, the church spire above the roofs. By the time I turned for home the sun was out, and the river looked like any other river again.

"There is a kind of friendship in that, built on nothing more than the same hour and the same path."

If you walk this path, go early. The best part of the day is over by eight.
