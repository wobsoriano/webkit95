City council approves new park on the old rail yard

By Dana Whitfield, City Hall reporter

Share this story: TwitterFacebookLinkedInEmail

The city council voted 7 to 2 on Tuesday night to turn the vacant rail yard on the east bank into a public park, ending a debate that has run for almost a decade. The plan sets aside eleven acres for open lawn, a playground and a riverside walking path, and keeps the old signal tower as a lookout.

Council member Rosa Delgado, who represents the east bank, said residents had waited long enough. She pointed to a survey in which more than two thirds of neighbors said the nearest green space was a twenty minute walk away. article-marker-5521 Supporters filled the chamber and the overflow room, and several spoke about children who now play in parking lots.

The two members who voted against the plan raised concerns about the cost of cleaning up the soil. Engineers estimate the cleanup at four million dollars, most of which the city expects to cover with a state brownfield grant. The remainder would come from the parks bond that voters approved two years ago.

Construction is expected to start next spring. The parks department will hold three public meetings this fall to gather ideas for the playground design and for the name of the park. Early suggestions include Signal Tower Park and a name honoring the rail workers who once worked the yard.

Local business owners along Front Street welcomed the decision. The owner of a bakery across from the rail yard said foot traffic could double once the path opens, and that she plans to add outdoor seating. Others asked the council to add bike racks and public restrooms, which the plan already includes.

The first phase, the lawn and the path, should open by the end of next year. The playground and the lookout tower would follow in the second phase, pending the final grant decision from the state.
